## Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles

Every Amazon interview loop, including technical rounds, weaves in behavioral questions explicitly
mapped to these principles. Strong answers use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
with a real, specific example — never a hypothetical or a "we" story that doesn't show individual
contribution.

### Customer Obsession
Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. Probes: a time you prioritized a
customer/user's real need over what was easier to build or ship.

### Ownership
Leaders act on behalf of the entire company, not just their team, and never say "that's not my
job." Probes: a time you took responsibility beyond your explicit task scope.

### Invent and Simplify
Leaders expect and require innovation, and simplify wherever possible. Probes: a time you
simplified something that was overly complex, or invented a new approach to a stale problem.

### Are Right, A Lot
Leaders have strong judgment and good instincts, seeking diverse perspectives to disconfirm their
beliefs. Probes: a decision you got right with limited data, and how you validated it.

### Learn and Be Curious
Leaders never stop learning and seek to improve themselves. Probes: a time you learned a new skill
or domain quickly to solve a problem, or a genuine failure and what you learned from it.

### Hire and Develop the Best
Leaders raise the performance bar and coach others. Probes: mentoring or reviewing others' work
(most relevant for candidates with some team/lead experience).

### Insist on the Highest Standards
Leaders have relentlessly high standards and drive teams to deliver quality. Probes: a time you
pushed back on shipping something you felt wasn't good enough.

### Think Big
Leaders create and communicate a bold direction. Probes: a time you proposed a larger or more
ambitious solution than what was initially asked for.

### Bias for Action
Speed matters; most decisions are reversible and don't need extensive study. Probes: a time you
made a decision with incomplete information rather than waiting for perfect data.

### Frugality
Accomplish more with less; constraints breed resourcefulness. Probes: a time you solved a problem
without additional budget, headcount, or tooling.

### Earn Trust
Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. Probes: a time you gave
or received difficult feedback.

### Dive Deep
Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to details, and audit frequently. Probes: the most
complex technical problem you've solved, with real depth expected on follow-up.

### Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
Leaders respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, but commit fully once a decision is
made. Probes: a time you disagreed with a manager or team and how you handled it afterward.

### Deliver Results
Leaders focus on key inputs and deliver with the right quality and in a timely fashion. Probes: a
time you delivered under a tight deadline — quantify the outcome where possible.

### Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
Leaders create a safer, more productive, higher-performing environment for their teams. Probes:
something you did to improve team morale, process, or working conditions (most relevant for
candidates with some team-leading experience).

### Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility
Leaders are aware of the broader impact of their actions. Probes: a time you considered the wider
consequences (ethical, safety, societal) of a technical decision.

### Preparation approach
Prepare 2-3 concrete STAR stories from real projects/internships, then map each one to whichever
principle(s) it best supports rather than writing one story per principle — most stories can
plausibly support 2-3 principles depending on how they're framed.
